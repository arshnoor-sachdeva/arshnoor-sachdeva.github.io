Overview

During my internship at Tesla's vehicle engineering group in Fremont, I worked within the Analysis Engineering team developing and running FEM models for chassis subsystems — evaluating durability, fatigue life, NVH performance, and structural integrity to support design decisions under program timing constraints.

The Problem

Chassis components must meet stringent durability and NVH targets across a wide range of real-world load cases. Identifying structural weaknesses and vibrational failure modes early in the design cycle is critical to avoiding costly late-stage redesigns. My work focused primarily on the durability team, running analyses on components ranging from brake pedals to motor mounts and door assemblies.

My Approach

For each subsystem, I owned the full FEA workflow — meshing parts in ANSA, assigning material properties, defining contacts and boundary conditions, and running simulations in ABAQUS using implicit static and modal solvers. For fatigue life work, I generated stress transfer functions in ABAQUS and used nCode Designlife for random vibration fatigue analysis under PSD loading. Results were analyzed, design changes proposed, and simulations iterated until targets were met.

Outcome

The brake pedal analysis evaluated deflections and stresses under sudden braking at both vehicle and brake assembly level. Results confirmed no plastic strain under peak loads, verifying structural integrity. A significant portion of total deflection was traced back to the surrounding dash panel — leading to targeted reinforcements that achieved a 30% stiffness improvement, resulting in lower displacement and more robust pedal feel.

Other Subsystems

Beyond the brake pedal, I contributed to analyses across a broad range of vehicle subsystems including motor mount strength load cases, steering gearbox modal analysis, fatigue life analysis of multiple brackets, fender stepping, center screen abuse, door abuse, and frunk load cases.
